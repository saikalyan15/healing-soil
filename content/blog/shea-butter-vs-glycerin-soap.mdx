These two bases sit at opposite ends of the feel spectrum. One is dense and conditioning, the other is light and clean-rinsing. Neither is better on its own; it depends on what kind of wash you prefer.

## What is a soap base?

A soap base is the core ingredient blend a bar is made from. It sets the lather, the texture on skin, and what the bar leaves behind after you rinse.

We use three soap bases: [glycerin](/ingredient/glycerin), [goat milk](/ingredient/goat-milk), and [shea butter](/ingredient/shea-butter). Shea butter and glycerin are the two furthest apart in feel.

## Shea butter soap base: rich and conditioning

Shea butter is the richest-feeling of the three bases. It creates a dense bar, a thick, creamy lather, and a conditioning after-wash feel rather than a squeaky-clean one.

It suits people who enjoy a rich wash and want skin to feel soft afterwards, including on drier patches.

## Glycerin soap base: light and clean

Glycerin is a natural by-product of soap-making. Many commercial manufacturers remove it to sell separately as a cosmetic ingredient; in our glycerin bars it stays in.

The bar is clear or semi-transparent, the lather is light, and the feeling after rinsing is clean without residue. It is a useful everyday bar for anyone who prefers an easy-rinsing wash, especially in warmer months.

## A quick way to decide

If you prefer the richest, most conditioning texture, start with shea butter.

If you prefer a light, easy-rinsing wash, start with glycerin.

Many people keep both: a shea butter bar for drier months or drier patches, a glycerin bar for everyday use in warmer weather.
