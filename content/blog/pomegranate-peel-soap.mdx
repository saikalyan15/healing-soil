Pomegranate peel is usually discarded after the fruit is eaten. For our pomegranate soap, the peel becomes a botanical ingredient with a warm colour and subtle natural texture.

![Pomegranate peels drying on a tray](/blog/pomegranate-peels-drying-tray.webp)

## Step 1: clean and dry the peel

The peel is cleaned, cut into smaller pieces, and spread in a thin layer to dry. Good airflow matters: the pieces need to become fully dry before grinding so the finished powder stores well and mixes evenly.

## Step 2: grind the dried peel

Once dry, the peel is ground into a fine botanical powder. The powder adds a muted earthy tone and a quiet fruit-and-plant character rather than a synthetic perfume.

## Step 3: choose the soap base

We make pomegranate soap in two versions:

- [Pomegranate glycerin soap](/shop/pomegranate-glycerin-soap) for a lighter lather and easy-rinsing feel.
- [Pomegranate goat milk soap](/shop/pomegranate-goat-milk-soap) for a creamier lather and richer feel.

Both are made without SLS, parabens, or synthetic fragrance.

## Step 4: mix and hand-pour

The soap base is melted slowly, the prepared pomegranate peel is mixed through the batch, and each bar is poured by hand. After setting, the bars are checked, packed, and prepared for dispatch from Goa.

## What the finished bar feels like

Choose glycerin if you like a light wash. Choose goat milk if you prefer a creamy texture and a more nourishing after-wash feel. Keep either bar on a draining soap dish so it can dry between uses.
