SLS-free soap is soap made without sodium lauryl sulfate, a synthetic detergent commonly used to create fast, dense foam.

Choosing an SLS-free bar is mainly an ingredient decision. The quickest way to verify it is to read the label rather than rely on words such as “natural” or “handmade.”

## What to look for on the label

Check the ingredient list for:

- Sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS)
- Sodium laureth sulfate (SLES)
- Sodium coco sulfate
- Ammonium lauryl sulfate

If none of these appear, the bar is free from those added sulfate detergents. You can also check whether the maker lists synthetic fragrance, usually shown as “fragrance” or “parfum.”

## Does SLS-free mean fragrance-free?

No. These are separate choices. A bar can be SLS-free and still contain synthetic fragrance.

Healing Soil bars contain no SLS, parabens, or synthetic fragrance. Their scent comes from the botanicals and essential oils listed for each product.

## How does SLS-free soap lather?

The lather comes from the soap base itself. It may feel creamier and quieter than the fast foam produced by a synthetic detergent bar.

Lather also varies by base:

- Glycerin produces a lighter, bubblier lather.
- Goat milk produces a soft, creamy lather.
- Shea butter produces a dense, rich lather.

Hard water, bar storage, and the amount of water used can also change the lather.

## Is every handmade soap SLS-free?

No. “Handmade” describes how the finished product is made; it does not guarantee a particular ingredient list. Some makers use bases containing added detergents.

Ask for the complete ingredient list and look for a clear SLS-free statement.
