Shea butter and goat milk are the two richest soap bases in the Healing Soil range. Both offer creamy lather and a moisturising feel, but they are not identical.

This comparison focuses on texture, scent, ingredients, and everyday use so you can choose by preference.

## Goat milk soap: creamy and versatile

[Goat milk soap](/goat-milk-soap) has an opaque appearance and a soft, creamy lather. It feels richer than glycerin but lighter than shea butter.

People with dry or sensitive skin types often prefer goat milk in traditional personal care. It is also a useful middle ground if you are new to handmade soap and do not yet know how rich you want the bar to feel.

## Shea butter soap: dense and rich

[Shea butter soap](/shea-butter-soap) has the densest lather of the three Healing Soil bases. The bar feels substantial in the hand and leaves a more conditioned after-wash feel.

People whose skin type tends to feel very dry or tight often prefer this richer texture, particularly in cooler or drier weather.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Goat milk soap | Shea butter soap |
|---|---|---|
| Lather | Creamy and soft | Dense and rich |
| Weight on skin | Medium-rich | Richest in the range |
| After-wash feel | Soft and nourishing | Soft and conditioned |
| Useful starting point | Sensitive or dry skin types | Very dry-feeling or mature skin types |
| Warm-weather feel | Lighter | May feel rich for some people |

## How to choose

Choose goat milk if you want a creamy daily bar with a medium-rich feel. Choose shea butter if you enjoy a denser lather and the richest after-wash feel.

If you remain unsure, try one of each and compare them over several washes. Soap preference is sensory: the lather, scent, and rinse feel matter as much as the ingredient name.

## What Healing Soil leaves out

Both bases are made without SLS, parabens, or synthetic fragrance. Their differences come from the base and the listed botanicals, not from added synthetic foaming agents.
