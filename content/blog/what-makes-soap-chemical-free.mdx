“Chemical-free soap” is a common search phrase, but it is not scientifically literal. Water, oils, glycerin, and soap itself are all chemicals.

What most shoppers mean is a bar with a short, transparent ingredient list and without selected synthetic additives such as SLS, parabens, or synthetic fragrance.

## Read the ingredient list first

Packaging words such as “natural,” “pure,” and “herbal” do not tell you everything in the bar. The ingredient list is more useful.

If you want a simpler formulation, look for:

- A clearly named soap base
- Oils, butters, milk, botanicals, or essential oils listed specifically
- No SLS or SLES
- No parabens
- No synthetic fragrance

“Fragrance” or “parfum” can refer to a formulated scent blend. If avoiding synthetic fragrance matters to you, ask the maker how the bar is scented.

## Technical names are not automatically a problem

Cosmetic labels often use INCI names, the standard international names for ingredients. Shea butter may appear as *Butyrospermum parkii butter*, and coconut oil as *Cocos nucifera oil*.

These names can look unfamiliar while still describing ordinary plant-derived ingredients. A transparent maker should be able to explain each item plainly.
