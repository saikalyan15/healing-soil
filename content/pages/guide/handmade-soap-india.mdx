## What a commercial bar is made of

Many commercial bars are built around synthetic foaming agents compressed into a bar. Soap formulas vary widely, so the useful habit is to read the whole label and not a single claim on the front.

Glycerine bases give a light, smooth lather. Goat milk bases feel creamier. Shea butter bases feel richer and more conditioning. SLS is one ingredient used in some products to make fast foam. Healing Soil bars do not contain it, but a fair comparison should still look at the entire formula.

## How to read a soap label

Indian labels follow INCI naming, so the same ingredient appears under the same name on every pack. Ingredients are listed from the highest amount to the lowest.

### What to check

- **Sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS)** and **sodium laureth sulfate (SLES)** are different surfactants. Check them separately.
- **Fragrance** or **parfum** are label terms for added scent that do not always name each scent material.
- **Parabens**, such as methylparaben and propylparaben, are synthetic preservatives.
- **Triclosan** is a synthetic additive found in some bars.

### Signs of a clear label

- A short list you can read aloud without stumbling.
- Plant ingredients named plainly, such as neem or tulsi, not "herbal blend".
- Glycerine named as an ingredient.

## The soap bases

Most handmade soap in India is built on a glycerine, goat milk or shea butter base. We also use a papaya cucumber base for people who avoid goat milk.

### Glycerine

A light lather and an easy-rinsing feel. Choose it for a smooth daily bar without the richness of goat milk or shea butter. See our [glycerine soaps](/glycerin-soap) and the post on [glycerine vs goat milk soap](/blog/glycerin-vs-goat-milk-soap).

### Goat milk

A creamy texture and a richer lather than glycerine. A good choice for dry and sensitive skin types, and for anyone who simply prefers a creamier wash. See our [goat milk soaps](/goat-milk-soap) and [goat milk soap: ingredients, lather and feel](/blog/goat-milk-soap-benefits).

### Shea butter

The richest of the bases: a thick, creamy lather and a conditioning feel. Choose it when you enjoy a fuller texture. See our [shea butter soap](/shea-butter-soap) and [shea butter in soap: what it does](/blog/understanding-the-benefits-of-shea-butter-in-soap).

## Climate and storage

Humid weather and dry weather change what feels good. In summer and monsoon, many people prefer a lighter glycerine or goat milk bar. In dry winter months, goat milk or shea butter feels more comfortable.

Keep the bar dry between washes. A draining dish or a hanging pouch lets the water run off. A bar left sitting in water goes soft and wears away faster. If you can, rotate between two bars and let each dry fully. Used this way, a single bar often lasts four to six weeks.
